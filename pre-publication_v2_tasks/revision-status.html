Read this first — three paragraphs, then the table makes sense

1. What the paper is

A controlled study of compressed-and-indexed attention for long-horizon time-series forecasting: instead of attending to every past timestep, the model compresses the key/value stream into blocks and routes each query to a few of them.

The study varies the two design knobs independently — how hard the stream is compressed (cKV) and how finely routing resolves (cR) — in a parameter-equalized 2×2 factorial on ETTh1 with 25 paired seeds, then repeats a three-way comparison on 12 datasets.

2. What it found

Every compressed configuration beat full attention on validation error (−18.8% to −27.0%), the advantage is signal-dependent (smooth, redundant series benefit; exact-recall synthetic probes do not), and coarsening routing hurts while coarsening compression mildly helps.

Two controls carry the attribution: a dense baseline retrained with a matched projection still loses (so the gap is not an artifact of unequal projections), and removing the router's selection at identical parameters costs 12–27% relative accuracy (so the router does real work).

3. What the review was

A 12-page AI-generated technical review (not referee correspondence) dated 2026-10-16, plus a manager's note about a journal target. It asks for corrections (R1–R8), extra controls and scope (C1–C6), and journal packaging (S1).

It found two real arithmetic defects and four overclaims — genuine value, all fixed. Its wider "minimum package" was estimated at 75–110 hours; the team froze a 10–15 hour subset, Option A, that closes every confound on the paper's own claims.

Glossary — the terms the table assumes

cKV / cR
Compression rate of the key/value stream / routing resolution (how many compressed blocks each query chooses among).
(4,4), (8,4)
A cell's setting, written (cKV, cR): e.g. (8,4) compresses the stream 8:1 and routes at 4:1.
CSA / DRRA
The paper's names for the equal-rate cell (4,4) and the dual-rate corner (8,4); CSA matches the design point of DeepSeek's Compressed Sparse Attention.
Index token
One entry in the compressed stream that queries are routed to; a block covers several timesteps.
Router / indexer
The scorer that ranks index tokens plus the top-k selector that keeps k of them. Non-learned here: the hard selection gives the scorer no task gradient.
All-block control
The same model with the selection removed (k = all index tokens): same parameters, same counted multiplies, no routing choice. It isolates what the router contributes.
Matched projection
A dense baseline whose output projection has the compressed cells' rank, so the comparison is not confounded by a wider projection in the dense model.
Validation / test MSE
Mean squared error in standardized units on held-out data; lower is better. Validation is the pre-registered metric, test is reported alongside.
Paired seed
Each of 25 seeds trains every cell on the identical data order and initialisation stream, so cells are compared within a seed, not across seeds.
Holm correction
A multiplicity adjustment across the pre-registered family of five contrasts; it makes the reported p-values conservative.
Analytic MAC count
Multiply–accumulate operations derived from tensor shapes, not measured with a profiler. It counts arithmetic, not memory traffic or wall-clock work.
Forecast-required path
Computing only the last position's output, which is all a forecaster needs; contrast with the full-layer path, which keeps every temporal output.
Realized blocks / Jaccard
How many distinct blocks a query actually reads after mapping / the overlap between two epochs' selected sets (1 = identical).
ETTh1
The Electricity Transformer Temperature benchmark, hourly, used as the frozen primary task: lookback 256, horizon 96.
MoBA / PatchTST
Mixture-of-block attention (nearest neighbour in design) / a patch-based transformer used as a transfer backbone, not a peer method.
Frozen protocol
Training settings fixed before runs (10 epochs, batch 32, learning rate 1e-4, identical schedule and auxiliary modules) so only the attention design varies.

What the paper does not claim — the boundaries a reader should know

Not a GPU result. All measurements are CPU-only, single machine, fp32, one small model scale. Energy figures are projections from the analytic account, not measurements.

Not an equivalence claim. No non-inferiority margin is asserted, so no “equivalent to dense” verdict is made; the all-block control reports a bound instead.

No mechanism claim. The probes are descriptive (two of them n=1) and the paper says a mechanism remains unverified.

Not universal. The advantage is signal-dependent: 8 of 12 datasets each for the two headline cells, with losses on exact-recall synthetic probes, exchange-rate validation and weather test error.

Not a latency win. Latency differences are directional only; on the forecast-required path the dense baseline is fastest.

Not a new mechanism. No component is claimed original; the contribution is the controlled design, the measured controls and the honest boundaries.

Why the deferred items were not run — the reasoning per item, not just a status

Two were narrowed instead of run (R3, R7): the paper states the boundary the experiment would have tested, at the cost of a paragraph. Three are scope expansions (C2, C3, C5): they add claims the paper does not make, so running them raises the bar without ever closing it. One (C6) is closed by text wherever the paper can state the answer, with the remaining part named as a protocol change rather than a correction. The rule applied — written down before any of this was decided, in N_necessity_audit.md — is: keep an item if it points at a claim the paper makes that is false, unverifiable from the artifacts, or confounded by something the paper itself discloses; drop it if it only adds a claim the paper never made. Each entry below says what the paper says instead and what would reopen it.

R3 — realized information budget narrowed

Fixed top-k means the cells read different amounts of history, so the KV contrast is a comparison of complete configurations, not of matched coverage. Making it a budget comparison needs a filling rule (D2) frozen before the run, and it is the largest item on the borderline list at 10–15 h. The venue call chose the bounded paper, so it was not bought.

Paper says instead: §4.2 “What the cKV contrast changes” — the effect is a statement about configuration at a fixed selection count, with budget matching named as the deferred next comparison. Reopens if: the target becomes a high-aim venue, or a referee asks whether the KV effect is a budget statement.

R7 — transfer routing saturation narrowed

Re-running costs 10–15 h and would only bound the same claim a second way. The free fix is the geometry disclosure: with 31 patch tokens and k=4, the coarse-router cells have three candidates and select all of them, so the tie cannot show that routing washes out on a stronger backbone.

Paper says instead: the tie is stated to bound the transfer claim from below — §4.6, the conclusion, Appendix C and the limitations all say it. Reopens if: the transfer is ever cited as evidence that rate effects vanish, which it no longer is.

C2 — generalization matrix scope

The manuscript already reports twelve datasets, 25 paired seeds on ETTh1 and a four-cell replication on three more datasets. Horizons, longer lookbacks and a second backbone are the grid of a different (practical-forecasting) paper; the review itself separates that track from the mechanism track this paper is in.

Paper says instead: §5.3 states the dataset-breadth limit and Appendix F names the horizon and lookback sweeps as remaining work. Reopens if: a referee asks for a longer lookback — the admissible response is one lookback on two datasets, not this matrix.

C3 — systems and energy scope

The paper claims neither GPU behaviour nor measured energy. It reports single-machine CPU latency with an explicitly directional stance, and a first-order arithmetic-to-energy projection whose assumptions are stated and whose scope is a closed form, not a measurement. A second platform or instrumented energy would test a claim the paper does not make.

Paper says instead: §4.9 and Appendix D state CPU-only, single machine, and “MACs are not energy”. Reopens if: a referee asks for systems evidence — wall clock under the same protocol is the cheap answer, energy only with instrumentation.

C5 — mechanism programme scope

The paper never claims a mechanism, and says so twice: the probes are labelled descriptive (n=1 seed for two of them) and §5.2 lists candidate explanations as unverified. Three controlled signal families plus a filter comparison plus an analytical model is a different paper's contribution, not a repair of this one.

Paper says instead: §4.4 “Mechanism probes (descriptive)”, §5.2 “Candidate explanations”, and the limitations. Reopens if: a referee says the mechanism is asserted rather than offered — the answer points at the explicit unverified statement.

C6 — inference: temporal uncertainty, MAE, margin narrowed

The protocol is a fixed chronological split with paired seeds, and the paper makes no equivalence claim — so a non-inferiority margin has nothing to protect. What can be closed by text is closed: the estimand is stated (seed-level paired mean differences), no equivalence test is run and the reason is given, and the MAE omission is stated. Rolling origins and a block bootstrap would be a protocol change, not a correction.

Paper says instead: §4.7 (deferred items with reasons), §5.3 “Non-IID samples”, and the new sentence in the limitations that only squared error is reported and no claim is made that the ordering survives another loss. Reopens if: a referee disputes the intervals or the word “tied”.
