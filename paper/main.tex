\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
Every number in this paper traces to a machine-readable artifact in the
repository: the frozen task specification, the pre-registered factorial
design, per-run JSONL manifests for all 125 factorial/baseline runs (including the
corrected-mapping re-runs of three cells), the
180-run cross-dataset suite, the external reference-point runs, the systems
benchmarks, and the causality/wiring-audit test suites
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-repro}). Figures and tables are regenerated by
scripts that re-read the same manifests and print verification lines
against the audited summaries. The seed lists for the power extension were
fixed in its design document before any extension run; the merge with the
pre-registered seeds was planned at that time
(Section~\ref{sec:stats}).

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This study uses public benchmark time-series datasets (cited in
Section~\ref{sec:task}) and deterministic synthetic generators; it involves
no human subjects and no sensitive data. The work is motivated by the
energy cost of attention at deployment scale (Section~\ref{sec:intro}):
by mapping which compression axis can be coarsened without accuracy loss,
it points toward cheaper long-horizon forecasting on power-constrained
hardware. That motivation is quantitative but indirect---the paper reports
single-machine latency and arithmetic counts, together with a first-order
projection of the energy consequences of the measured work reductions
under stated assumptions, rather than energy measurements, and it makes
no claim about specific production systems. All findings are scoped to the
frozen task and CPU scale (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
