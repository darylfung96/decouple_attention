\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon time-series forecasting with Transformers is dominated by the
cost of quadratic attention over long lookback windows. Compressed-and-indexed
attention---mechanisms that compress the key/value (KV) stream and route each
query to a small set of compressed blocks---promises to reduce this cost, but
its design space is rarely studied systematically. We treat this family as a
two-axis design problem: the KV compression rate $c_{KV}$ and the routing
resolution $c_R$. On the ETTh1 dataset under a frozen direct-96-step
forecasting protocol, we evaluate all four combinations of
$c_{KV}, c_R \in \{4{:}1, 8{:}1\}$ in a controlled, parameter-equalized $2
\times 2$ factorial design with five paired seeds. We report three findings.
First, \emph{every} compressed configuration outperforms full causal
attention on validation MSE ($-18.5\%$ to $-29.1\%$, and in 20/20 paired
seed comparisons) at an equal or slightly smaller parameter count; a
bit-exact wiring audit confirms the full-attention baseline is correctly
implemented. Second, coarsening the router ($c_R$: $4{:}1 \rightarrow 8{:}1$)
systematically deteriorates accuracy ($+0.046$ validation MSE), while
coarsening KV compression ($c_{KV}$: $4{:}1 \rightarrow 8{:}1$) is mildly
beneficial ($-0.043$): directional evidence that routing granularity, not KV
content density, carries accuracy. Third, the best cell $(c_{KV}{=}8, c_R{=}4)$
reduces the attention matrix to 48 columns versus 256 for full attention
(5.3$\times$) at a matching parameter count. All statistics are descriptive
(five seeds; no significance tests); multi-dataset, significance, and
latency evaluations are ongoing work.
\end{abstract}
