\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon time-series forecasting with Transformers is dominated by the
cost of quadratic attention over long lookback windows. Compressed-and-indexed
attention---compressing the key/value (KV) stream and routing each query to a
few compressed blocks---reduces this cost, but its design space is rarely
studied systematically. We vary two axes: the KV compression rate $c_{KV}$ and
the routing resolution $c_R$. Under a frozen direct-96-step protocol with
twenty-five paired seeds, we run a parameter-equalized $2\times2$ factorial on
ETTh1 and a three-way comparison---full attention, the equal-rate $(4,4)$
configuration (DeepSeek-V4's Compressed Sparse Attention design point), and the
dual-rate $(8,4)$ corner---across twelve datasets. We report four findings.
First, on ETTh1 every compressed configuration outperforms full causal
attention on validation mean squared error (MSE; mean deltas $-18.8\%$ to
$-27.0\%$, 95/100 paired comparisons, Holm-adjusted $p \le 3.3\times10^{-6}$)
at equal or smaller parameter count; removing the routing selection costs
$12$--$27\%$ relative accuracy at identical parameters and multiply--accumulate
(MAC) count, yet the selection-free cells still beat full attention. Second,
coarsening the router ($+0.030$ validation MSE) hurts while coarsening KV
compression is mildly beneficial ($-0.032$), supporting the decoupling
hypothesis (paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.0074$); a conditional reading localizes the
effect to the fine-content/coarse-router cell, and a three-dataset
replication did not reproduce the sign structure. Third, the dual-rate corner
reduces the attention matrix to 48 (+1 sink) columns versus 256
($5.22\times$) at matching parameters. Fourth, the advantage is signal-dependent:
smoother, more redundant series benefit most. Routing is a non-learned hard
top-$k$ selector, so no task gradient reaches the indexer and the effects are
architectural. Conclusions are established at $n{=}25$ on ETTh1; the
cross-dataset pattern is descriptive and latency is single-machine.
\end{abstract}
