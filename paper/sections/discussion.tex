\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The decoupling insight (directional)}
The cleanest reading of the factorial is that the two axes play different
roles: \emph{routing granularity carries accuracy; KV content density only
buys efficiency}. The control logic is internal to the design: the $(8,4)$
cell (coarse content, fine router) matches or beats the equal-rate cells
with half the attention columns, while the $(4,8)$ cell (fine content,
coarse router) is the worst cell despite having the same columns as $(4,4)$.
The per-seed consistency (7/10 in the same direction for each effect) and
the effect-size-to-seed-noise comparison (both effects ${\sim}0.04$ vs.\
within-cell std ${\sim}0.065$) mean we currently report this as a
\emph{directional} finding; significance testing and a multi-dataset
confirmation are the immediate next steps before this can be stated as a
general design principle.

\subsection{Why might compression help on this task?}
We do not have a verified explanation for the compressed $>$ full-attention
outcome, and we explicitly do not claim one. Candidate (unverified)
explanations include: compression acting as an inductive-bias regularizer;
the block structure effectively smoothing the attention field over
multi-step correlations; and the fixed 10-epoch budget interacting with
optimization landscape differences. Each is testable, and the
pre-registered hypothesis program in the project plan is designed to
separate them \todo{H1--H5 status per plan/paper\_notes.md \S 5; mechanistic
analyses are pending}.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single dataset.} All results are on ETTh1 (7 features, the
        smallest of the ETT family) at CPU-scale model sizes. No
        cross-dataset or cross-scale generalization claim is made.
        \todo{Gate 3: ETTh2/ETTm1/ETTm2, Electricity, Traffic, Weather,
        Exchange; horizons $\{96, 192, 336, 720\}$}.
  \item \textbf{Descriptive statistics.} Five paired seeds; no significance
        tests, confidence intervals beyond the reported std, or equivalence
        tests. ``Best on mean'' and ``directional'' are used advisedly.
  \item \textbf{No external baselines.} Comparisons are internal to the
        family plus the full-attention same-backbone baseline; PatchTST,
        iTransformer, DLinear, Informer, and linear/SSM baselines under the
        same protocol are pending \todo{Gate 3 baselines}.
  \item \textbf{No latency or hardware results} for the frozen protocol;
        complexity claims are structural only \todo{Gate 5}.
  \item \textbf{Non-IID samples.} Stride-1 windows overlap heavily; samples
        are not independent, which standard significance tests would
        assume \todo{Gate 4: block bootstrap / HAC-robust tests}.
  \item \textbf{Fixed budget, single checkpoint policy.} 10 epochs,
        epoch-10 weights; no early stopping or best-validation selection.
  \item \textbf{CPU-only, fp32, small models.} Dense-with-masking
        implementation choices are CPU-oriented and may not transfer to
        GPU-flash-attention regimes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What this paper does not claim}
Consistent with the project's pre-registered warnings: this is not a novel
sparse-attention mechanism; it is not a state-of-the-art forecasting
system; the word ``equivalence'' is not used for any accuracy comparison;
no subquadratic claim is made beyond the structural column counts above;
and no component is claimed to be original. The contribution is the
controlled study, its measurements, and its honest boundaries.
