\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Long-horizon time-series forecasting---predicting the next $H$ steps from a
lookback window of length $L$---is a core task in energy, traffic, and
finance applications. Transformer-based forecasting models \citep{zhou2021informer,nie2023patchtst,liu2024itransformer}
are accurate but pay a quadratic cost $O(L^2)$ in the attention mechanism as
the lookback grows. One widely used remedy is \emph{compressed-and-indexed}
attention: compress the $L$ key/value (KV) positions into $C = L / c_{KV}$
blocks, and have each query attend to only a small, \emph{routed} subset of
those blocks plus a local sliding window. This family underlies recent
efficient-attention designs for large language models
\citep{yuan2025nsa,deepseekv4_2026}, and its components---content-based
routing \citep{roy2020routing}, low-rank KV compression
\citep{deepseekv2_2024}---are individually well known.

The efficiency stakes are concrete. In large-scale ML, arithmetic work is
now the dominant driver of the energy and carbon cost of training and
inference \citep{strubell2019energy,patterson2021carbon,schwartz2020green,wu2022sustainable},
and energy is, to a first order, proportional to that work. Dense attention
sits on the steepest part of this curve: its dominant term is quadratic in
the lookback, so each doubling of $L$ quadruples the attention-layer
arithmetic---and, at a fixed hardware generation, the energy per window---
until a routing or compression remedy is applied. Long-horizon forecasting
turns this from an abstraction into an operating constraint: its
deployments (grid and building energy management, traffic control,
financial risk systems) are served continuously at sustained query volume,
often on power-constrained edge hardware, so per-forecast energy is a real
cost rather than an afterthought. For this family, efficiency is therefore
not a ``nice to have'': it is the difference between a forecasting system
that can and one that cannot be deployed at the volumes real applications
demand. The design question this paper measures---\emph{which compression
axis buys efficiency, and which axis, when coarsened, is the accuracy we
silently give away}---is thus also an energy question, and its answer
determines how cheaply accurate long-horizon forecasting can be run. We
return to that question quantitatively in Section~\ref{sec:forecast},
where the audited component accounting is used to forecast attention work
at contexts up to $2^{17}$ tokens and to project energy savings under
explicit first-order assumptions.

What is not well studied is the \emph{design space} of this family: how the
two independent compression axes interact. We identify two axes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{KV compression rate} $c_{KV}$: how aggressively the content
        stream is compressed into blocks ($C = L / c_{KV}$ blocks);
  \item \textbf{Routing resolution} $c_R$: how finely the router represents
        the history when deciding which blocks to attend to
        ($M = L / c_R$ index tokens).
\end{itemize}
Coarsening either axis cuts compute, but the two axes may not trade
accuracy equally. Our central question is therefore a controlled, empirical
one: \emph{which axis carries accuracy, and which axis only buys
efficiency?}

This paper is a \textbf{design study}, not a proposal of a new attention
mechanism: every building block we use is a configuration of known ideas
(Section~\ref{sec:method}), and overlaps with concurrent work
\citep{yuan2025nsa,deepseekv4_2026} are acknowledged rather than elided
(Section~\ref{sec:related}). Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] \textbf{A controlled factorial design with exploratory extensions.} All four
        combinations of $c_{KV}, c_R \in \{4{:}1, 8{:}1\}$ are evaluated on a
        frozen task---ETTh1, direct 96-step forecasting---under an
        \emph{auxiliary-identical} contract (identical window, top-$k$, heads,
        hidden size, optimizer, schedule) with attention-parameter
        equalization to within $0.3\%$, across 25 paired seeds
        (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}); the three-way comparison (full
        attention vs.\ the equal-rate and dual-rate cells) extends to twelve
        datasets (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}), and two deeper-ratio probes, $(16,4)$ and
        $(32,4)$, are reported separately as exploratory (Section~\ref{sec:rq1},
        Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-exploratory}).
  \item[(b)] \textbf{A decoupled-rate finding.} Coarsening the
        router deteriorates accuracy ($+0.030$ validation MSE for
        $c_R{:}4{:}1 \to 8{:}1$), while coarsening KV compression is
        mildly beneficial ($-0.032$); the deterioration is concentrated in the
        $(4,8)$ cell (fine content, coarse router), so the finding is stated
        conditionally rather than as a general rule about routing.
        The pre-registered decoupling
        hypothesis---the routing effect exceeds the KV effect on per-seed
        paired differences---is supported at $n{=}25$ (exact Wilcoxon
        $p = 0.0074$). The indexer receives no task gradient (hard top-$k$ is
        non-differentiable), so the effect is architectural, not learned; route
        membership still follows the drift of upstream features. The dual-rate corner
        $(c_{KV}{=}8, c_R{=}4)$ (\emph{DRRA},
        Section~\ref{sec:method}), reduces the attention matrix 5.22$\times$
        relative to full attention
        at equal parameters; its edge over the equal-rate cell is
        not significant on validation (exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.62$).
  \item[(c)] \textbf{A measured efficiency/accuracy characterization.} A
        surprising---and wiring-verified---baseline result: compressed
        configurations beat full causal attention on the frozen task
        (mean deltas $-18.8\%$ to $-27.0\%$; Holm-adjusted
        $p \le 3.3\times10^{-6}$ at $n{=}25$), and a projection-matched dense
        retrain does not close that gap; the router is load-bearing---removing
        its selection costs $12$--$27\%$ relative accuracy at identical
        parameters and MAC count---and the result is bounded by explicit
        limitations (CPU-scale models; across twelve datasets the
        advantage is signal-dependent, Section~\ref{sec:rq3}; external
        architectures are reference points, not peers,
        Section~\ref{sec:refpoints}).
  \item[(d)] \textbf{Reproducibility artifacts.} A frozen task specification,
        machine-readable results manifests, a verified parameter registry,
        causality-verification suites, and a codebase in which every reported
        number traces to a file (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-repro}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scope and non-claims.} This work does not claim a novel attention
mechanism, does not claim state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy (the
external architectures we ran are reference points for calibrating absolute
error levels, not peer methods; Section~\ref{sec:refpoints}),
and does not claim statistical equivalence. Wall-clock latency \emph{is}
reported---as development-machine measurements with a directional-only stance
(Table~\ref{tab:speed}, with paired and forecast-required companions in
Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}); cross-hardware transfer remains future work.
Analytic MACs are likewise reported with explicit caveats (the wrapper MLP
dominates; MACs are not energy). Pre-freeze latency figures from earlier stages
of this project are superseded and are deliberately \emph{not} used here.
