\section{Documented negative results}
\label{sec:appendix-negatives}
Six failed designs were tried and documented during the development of this
family (each with runnable or archived implementations and failure modes):
sparse-gather routing ($\sim$5$\times$ slower than dense masking on CPU);
a hierarchical (multi-scale pyramid) indexer (consistently worse than the
flat indexer); a multi-scale KV pyramid at 224 KV tokens
(overparameterized for the budget); a learnable block-bias offset (NaN
instability in $\sim$50\% of runs; root cause later traced to uninitialized
memory---`torch.empty' biases---and fixed); a linear-pool indexer with
gated residual KV (loses the per-head $\times$ per-dimension routing
flexibility of the softmax-overlap gate); and stacked $2{:}1 \rightarrow
2{:}1$ compression (harder to optimize within the budget). Negative results
of this kind are a deliberate part of the reproducibility package; they are
secondary to the paper's core claim.

\section{Exploratory rate sweep}
\label{sec:appendix-exploratory}
Two post-hoc cells, $(16,4)$ and $(32,4)$ (25 ETTh1 seeds each, same protocol),
probe how far KV compression goes while routing stays fine. They are kept out of the
confirmatory tables and figures by design; Figure~\ref{fig:exploratory} shows them
together with the factorial cells, marked with an asterisk. $(16,4)$ reaches the best mean
($0.5810$ validation, $0.8100$ test) and $(32,4)$ lands inside the top group ($0.6122$ /
$0.8586$): the KV-rate profile stays flat-to-beneficial out to 32:1 on ETTh1, and
4 KV blocks is the sensible floor where this study stops. Neither probe is
statistically separated from the named dual-rate corner in paired $25$-seed
contrasts: for $(16,4)$ versus $(8,4)$ the paired validation difference is
$-0.038$ (uncorrected exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.08$, $16$ of $25$ seeds favoring
$(16,4)$) and the test difference is $-0.034$ ($p = 0.06$, $16$ of $25$); for
$(32,4)$ the corresponding differences are $-0.007$ ($p = 0.51$) and $+0.015$
($p = 0.41$). The probes therefore remain exploratory: they motivate the
long-context forecast of Section~\ref{sec:forecast}, but they enter no
confirmatory claim, and promoting either to the named design corner would
require independent replication (fresh seeds and the cross-dataset suite,
as itemized in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-roadmap}).

The same four cells were also grafted onto the published PatchTST backbone
(ETTh1/ETTh2, five seeds each) as a transfer test. Table~\ref{tab:transfer} shows
all four tied within noise on both datasets. The transfer is
boundary-limited, and the tie does not by itself show that a strong backbone
washes out routing: with lookback 256, patch length 16 and stride 8 the
backbone carries 31 tokens, so at $c_R{=}4$ there are 6--7 candidate index
tokens and at $c_R{=}8$ only 3 against $k{=}4$---the coarse-router cells
select every candidate, and the fine-router cells select 4 of 6--7, which at
$c_{KV}{=}8$ map onto at most three coarse blocks. Routing resolution is
therefore barely exercised in this transfer, so the tie is consistent both
with backbone-dependence of the rate effects and with exaggerated routing
saturation at this token count; it bounds the transfer claim from below.
Figure~\ref{fig:transfer-speed} shows the same tie on the accuracy axis with separation on the speed axis.

\section{Energy projection: derivation, sensitivity, and audit}
\label{sec:appendix-energy}

This appendix documents the projection used in Section~\ref{sec:forecast}:
the operation counts it reads, its sensitivity to the
model's assumptions, and its checks against the audited single-machine
measurements. The purpose is to make every projected number in the main
text traceable to a released artifact and to state plainly what the
projection does and does not support.

\paragraph{Operation counts.} Table~\ref{tab:energy-components} lists the
per-component forward arithmetic of the three named configurations at
$L{=}256$, in millions of multiply--accumulate operations per sequence.
These are the released component accounts: the analytic totals in the last
row equal the audited totals exactly ($76.86$M, $63.30$M, and $61.20$M for
Normal, CSA $(4,4)$, and DRRA $(8,4)$), and the figure script asserts this
equality before drawing Figure~\ref{fig:scaling-forecast}. The router row
counts what the module executes: the routing query is the main multi-head
query and the index stream is single-headed, so the score matmul runs at
$[B, H, S, d_h] \times [B, 1, d_h, M]$, i.e.\ $h d_h = d = 128$ MACs per
(query, index-token) pair, as verified against the executed tensor shapes
in the released accounting log. Two asymmetries
between the columns are properties of the as-run configurations, not of
the projection: the dense baseline uses a rank-128 output projection while
the compressed cells use the equalized rank-64 bottleneck, and only the
compressed cells pay the router and compressor terms. Of the $15.66$M
whole-model gap between Normal and DRRA $(8,4)$, $10.49$M is the output
projection alone; matching that rank changes the dense account from $76.86$M to
$66.38$M and leaves a $7.8\%$ whole-model
difference. The matched baseline was itself trained end to end (25 paired seeds), not
merely re-accounted: it improves dense accuracy over the as-run Normal, yet
every compressed cell still beats it, so the accuracy gap is not a projection
artifact. Operations excluded
from the accounting (RMS normalization, rotary embeddings, mask
construction, the head-mixing weight of the index scores, and the softmax
normalization itself) are negligible at
these dimensions and are excluded consistently across configurations.

\paragraph{Derivation.} The derivation of Equations~\ref{eq:cf-work}--\ref{eq:cf-whole}---each factor's origin, each term's power of $L$, and the form of the whole-layer ratio---is given with the equations in the main text (Section~\ref{sec:forecast}). This appendix supplies the audit layer around it: the released operation counts (Table~\ref{tab:energy-components}), the sensitivity band, the validation checks, and the scope statement.

\paragraph{Sensitivity.} Three perturbations bound the assumptions.
(i)~\emph{Auxiliary weighting.} Reweighting the router and compressor
terms by a common factor $\lambda$ leaves the $32$k DRRA attention-path
saving at $81.1\%$ ($\lambda{=}0.5$), $74.7\%$ ($\lambda{=}1$), and
$61.9\%$ ($\lambda{=}2$): the direction is unchanged, and the spread sets
how much of the result rests on how the two auxiliary terms are costed.
(ii)~\emph{Single-head indexer.} The executed router scores each index
token with every head of the shared query, at $h d_h = d$ MACs per
comparison, so the frozen $h{=}4$ geometry pays four times what a
single-head indexer would; a single-head indexer (as in a
Lightning-Indexer-style design, which this module does not implement)
would raise the $32$k DRRA projection from $74.7\%$ to $84.0\%$ and the
asymptote from $75.0\%$ to $84.4\%$. The executed
arrangement is therefore the conservative one for the saving claim, and
the asymptotic router share $1/(2 c_R)$ does not shrink with the head
count at fixed $d$.
(iii)~\emph{Memory traffic.} The key/value traffic of the attention core
scales with the same selected-column count as its arithmetic, so an
additive energy cost per read column changes the effective coefficient
but not the column ratio; the structurally distinct terms are the router
(quadratic in $L$, with constant $1/c_R$) and the compressors (linear in
$L$), which the auxiliary-weighting band covers. Excluded from the model
throughout: memory stalls, frequency scaling under load, leakage, and
idle power. A complete energy evaluation would need per-level memory
counters and platform power traces; we report none and claim none.

\paragraph{Validation.} Three checks are available from the released
measurements. First, exact arithmetic: the analytic totals reproduce the
audited component accounts at $L{=}256$ exactly, as asserted by the figure
script. Second, rank agreement with time: across the audited lookback
sweep, measured forward latency ranks with analytic attention-path work at
Spearman $\rho = 1.000$ (batch 32, $n{=}15$) and $\rho = 0.992$
(batch 1, $n{=}18$), and the paired DRRA-versus-Normal saving ratio
correlates with the work ratio at Pearson $r = 0.96$ ($n{=}5$, batch 32).
Third, magnitude agreement where commensurate: the whole-layer projection
for the study's backbone is $50.5\%$ at $L{=}2048$/batch 32 against
$56.2\%$ measured, and $60.8\%$ at $L{=}4096$/batch 1 against $63.3\%$
measured. The measured saving exceeds the arithmetic projection at both
points, consistently with the dense baseline being memory-bound at these
contexts (its materialized score tensor dominates its time), so wall-clock
cost grows faster than MAC count there; the work model excludes memory
traffic by construction.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.} The projection answers ``how much
arithmetic does the design remove,'' not ``how many joules does it save.''
Under the stated first-order model the answer is hardware-independent; on
any real hardware the realized saving will be smaller wherever memory or
fixed costs dominate, as the batch-1 measurements already show. No claim
is made about GPUs, accelerators, or production systems.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:appendix-roadmap}
Scope completed in this study versus open follow-ups, by axis:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Statistics.} Factorial-level inference is complete:
        $n{=}25$ paired seeds; paired Wilcoxon with
        Holm correction over cell-vs-Normal contrasts
        (Section~\ref{sec:stats}); per-seed main-effect contrast for H1.
        Deferred with justification: mixed-effects model (the $2\times2$ already
        identifies the three fixed effects; deferred as a robustness re-expression),
        HAC/block bootstrap (seed-level analyses), TOST/ROPE (no
        equivalence claims); temporal-dependence-aware statistics for the
        cross-dataset suite remain future work. Realized-budget control at matched unique blocks, and any test-primary/MAE re-headline, are deferred by design (reported as measured, with test alongside validation; per-seed values in the released manifests).
  \item \textbf{Datasets.} The suite covers
        the ETT family (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2), large multivariate
        (Electricity, Traffic, Weather), different dynamics (Exchange,
        Solar-Energy), and three mechanistic synthetic families (sinusoids,
        change points, delayed-copy), all under the frozen protocol with 5
        paired seeds (180 runs; per-seed tables in the released results
        manifests). Remaining: horizon ($H \in \{192, 336, 720\}$)
        and lookback ($L \in \{512, 720, 1024\}$) sweeps. Copy-vs-noise isolation with fresh random content, one-factor smoothness/noise/localization sweeps with a selection-guidance figure, and independent confirmatory replication of the exploratory (16,4)/(32,4) probes (fresh seeds plus cross-dataset) are deferred to follow-up work.
  \item \textbf{Baselines.} DLinear \citep{zeng2023dlinear} and PatchTST \citep{nie2023patchtst}
        under the identical frozen protocol as external reference points,
        not peer methods (Section~\ref{sec:refpoints}): DLinear on all
        twelve datasets; PatchTST on the eight datasets where its
        channel-independent training fits the single-machine CPU budget
        (declared omissions on solar/electricity/traffic/weather).
        Remaining as future work: iTransformer, Informer, TimesNet, and an
        SSM baseline. All-block (selection-free) compression is now reported in Section~\ref{sec:stats}; the remaining diagnostic controls at matched budget (window-only, fixed/random routing) are deferred.
  \item \textbf{Hardware/latency.} Development-machine measurement is complete:
        Mean/p90/p99 forward latency with bootstrap
        CIs, throughput, analytic MACs, peak-memory accounting, training
        time, and batch/lookback scaling sweeps (batch 1--256,
        $L \in \{128, \dots, 4096\}$); see Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}.
        Remaining as future work: cross-hardware transfer (x86 CPU, GPU)
        and depth/width ablations --- conscious scope exclusions of this
        study.
  \item \textbf{Literature re-audit:} the 2026-08-16 sweep of the
        2024--2026 routing/compression-attention literature found no
        counterexample to the \emph{to our knowledge} claim
        (Section~\ref{sec:related}); a fresh sweep before locking the
        title remains good practice.
\end{enumerate}
