\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a controlled $2\times2$ factorial design study of
compressed-and-indexed attention for long-horizon time-series forecasting,
varying the KV compression rate $c_{KV}$ and the routing resolution $c_R$
independently under a frozen task, an auxiliary-identical contract, and
parameter equalization, across five paired seeds. On ETTh1 direct-96
forecasting, all compressed configurations outperformed full causal
attention ($-18.5\%$ to $-29.1\%$ validation MSE; 20/20 paired seed
comparisons; wiring-audited baseline), and the factorial separates the two
axes directionally: coarser routing deteriorates accuracy while coarser KV
compression is mildly beneficial, with the champion cell DRRA $(8,4)$ cutting
the attention matrix 5.3$\times$ at equal parameters. Replicating the
three-way comparison across twelve datasets shows the advantage is
\emph{signal-dependent}, not universal: the champion beats full attention on
mean validation MSE in 8/12 datasets (CSA $(4,4)$ in 7/12), with
the wins concentrated on the ETT family, solar, and electricity, and slight
losses on the mechanistic synthetic probes, exchange-rate validation, and
weather test error. We deliberately stop short of significance,
generalization, or latency claims: the cross-dataset pattern is descriptive
and characterized by dataset, not asserted as a design law.

These results also carry a deployment-facing reading. The champion's
structural savings---$5.3\times$ fewer attention columns and $22\%$ fewer
forward MACs than full attention at equal or fewer parameters---mean that
per-forecast arithmetic work is roughly a fifth smaller on the frozen task,
and energy tracks arithmetic to a first order
\citep{patterson2021carbon,wu2022sustainable}; on hardware where the
attention term dominates, the gap only widens as the lookback grows
(Figure~\ref{fig:lscale}). We do not claim that translation here: the
measured latency advantage is directional and single-machine, and
cross-hardware transfer remains open. But the efficiency gradient this
paper maps is the one that matters operationally: content density can be
coarsened without directionally costing accuracy on the smooth, redundant
series that dominate production forecasting (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).

The immediate
roadmap is statistical testing with temporal-dependence-aware methods,
external baselines, and hardware latency benchmarking; each is itemized in
the appendix roadmap (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-roadmap}).
