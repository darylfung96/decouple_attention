\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a controlled $2\times2$ factorial design study of
compressed-and-indexed attention for long-horizon time-series forecasting,
varying the KV compression rate $c_{KV}$ and the routing resolution $c_R$
independently under a frozen task, an auxiliary-identical contract, and
parameter equalization, across 25 paired seeds. On ETTh1 direct-96
forecasting, every compressed configuration outperformed full causal
attention (mean validation deltas $-16.6\%$ to $-26.2\%$; 96/100 paired
seed comparisons; Holm-adjusted $p \approx 7\times10^{-5}$;
wiring-audited baseline), and the factorial separates the two axes:
coarser routing deteriorates accuracy while coarser KV compression is
mildly beneficial, with the pre-registered decoupling hypothesis supported
on per-seed paired contrasts ($p = 0.0032$) and the champion cell DRRA
$(8,4)$ cutting the attention matrix 5.3$\times$ at equal parameters (its
edge over the equal-rate cell remains directional, $p = 0.093$).
Replicating the three-way comparison across twelve datasets shows the
advantage is \emph{signal-dependent}, not universal: the champion beats
full attention on mean validation MSE in 8/12 datasets (CSA $(4,4)$ in
7/12), with the wins concentrated on the ETT family, solar, and
electricity, and slight losses on the mechanistic synthetic probes,
exchange-rate validation, and weather test error. We deliberately stop
short of generalization or latency claims: the factorial conclusions are
established on one task, the cross-dataset pattern is descriptive and
dataset-characterized, not asserted as a design law.

These results also carry a deployment-facing reading. The champion's
structural savings---$5.3\times$ fewer attention columns and $22\%$ fewer
forward MACs than full attention at equal or fewer parameters---mean that
per-forecast arithmetic work is roughly a fifth smaller on the frozen task,
and energy tracks arithmetic to a first order
\citep{patterson2021carbon,wu2022sustainable}; on hardware where the
attention term dominates, the gap only widens as the lookback grows
(Figure~\ref{fig:lscale}). We do not claim that translation here: the
measured latency advantage is directional and single-machine, and
cross-hardware transfer remains open. But the efficiency gradient this
paper maps is the one that matters operationally: content density can be
coarsened without directionally costing accuracy on the smooth, redundant
series that dominate production forecasting (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).

The immediate
roadmap is temporal-dependence-aware statistics for the cross-dataset
suite, efficiency-oriented peer baselines, and hardware latency
benchmarking; each is itemized in
the appendix roadmap (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-roadmap}).
