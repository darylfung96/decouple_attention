\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a controlled $2\times2$ factorial design study of
compressed-and-indexed attention for long-horizon time-series forecasting,
varying the KV compression rate $c_{KV}$ and the routing resolution $c_R$
independently under a frozen task, an auxiliary-identical contract, and
parameter equalization, across 25 paired seeds. On ETTh1 direct-96
forecasting, every compressed configuration outperformed full causal
attention (mean validation deltas $-18.8\%$ to $-27.0\%$; 95/100 paired
seed comparisons; Holm-adjusted $p \le 3.3\times10^{-6}$;
wiring-audited baseline), and the factorial separates the two axes:
coarser routing deteriorates accuracy while coarser KV compression is
mildly beneficial, with the pre-registered decoupling hypothesis supported
on per-seed paired contrasts (exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.0074$) and its interaction
localized to the $(4,8)$ cell (Section~\ref{sec:rq1}); DRRA
$(8,4)$ cuts the attention matrix 5.22$\times$ (256 vs.\ 49 complete columns) at equal parameters (its
edge over the equal-rate cell is not significant on validation, exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.62$).
The attribution is measured rather than assumed: with the selection removed at
identical parameters and MAC count, accuracy falls $12$--$27\%$ relative, while the
selection-free cells still beat full attention
(Table~\ref{tab:allblock}).
Replicating the three-way comparison across twelve datasets shows the
advantage is \emph{signal-dependent}, not universal: DRRA $(8,4)$ and CSA $(4,4)$ beat
full attention on mean validation MSE in 8/12 datasets each, with the wins concentrated on the ETT family, solar, and
electricity, and slight losses on the mechanistic synthetic probes,
exchange-rate validation, and weather test error. We deliberately stop
short of generalization or latency claims: the factorial conclusions are
established on one task, the cross-dataset pattern is descriptive and
dataset-characterized, not asserted as a design law. The full four-cell replication on three contrasting datasets did not reproduce the ETTh1 sign structure, exploratory (16,4) and (32,4) probes stay flat-to-beneficial out to 32:1 outside the confirmatory tables, and a PatchTST-backbone transfer ties all four cells within noise, so rate effects are backbone-relative (that transfer's 31-token tokenization leaves the coarse-router cells selecting every candidate, so it bounds the claim from below). The indexer receives no task gradient, so granularity effects are architectural.

These results also carry a deployment-facing reading. DRRA $(8,4)$'s
structural savings---$5.22\times$ fewer attention columns and $20\%$ fewer
forward MACs than full attention at equal or fewer parameters (projection-matched,
$7.8\%$; the dense baseline's rank-128 output projection accounts for two-thirds of the
whole-model gap), and matching that projection narrows the arithmetic gap but
not the accuracy gap: the retrained matched baseline scores $0.79$ validation
MSE, better than the as-run Normal, and every compressed cell still beats it
(Section~\ref{sec:stats}). So
per-forecast arithmetic work is roughly a fifth smaller on the frozen task at
batched operating points (at batch 1 the dense baseline remains fastest,
Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}, so this reading does not extend to single-window
edge inference); where attention dominates the arithmetic, energy tracks it to a
first order \citep{patterson2021carbon,wu2022sustainable}, subject to the
MACs-are-not-energy caveat in Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}; on hardware where the
attention term dominates, the gap only widens as the lookback grows
(Figure~\ref{fig:lscale}). Two structural facts make the aggregate stakes
larger than any single measurement: forecasting services run continuously
at sustained query volume, so per-forecast arithmetic savings compound with
volume rather than accruing once; and because the saving is architectural,
it is inherited by any deployment that adopts the same design point,
independent of software stack. We do not claim that translation here: the
measured latency advantage is directional and single-machine (both the
full-layer and the forecast-required paths are reported,
Figure~\ref{fig:perf-speed}), and
cross-hardware transfer remains open. But the efficiency gradient this
paper maps is the one that matters operationally: content density can be
coarsened without directionally costing accuracy on the smooth, redundant
series that dominate production forecasting (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).

The scaling forecast in Figure~\ref{fig:scaling-forecast} extends this
reading beyond the measured range. Under the audited component accounting,
DRRA $(8,4)$ removes $74.7\%$ of attention-path work at a $32$k-token
context---rising to a $42.7$--$72.9\%$ reduction of whole-layer work at
widths from $4096$ down to $128$---and the closed form
$S_\infty = 1 - (1/c_{KV} + 1/(2 c_R))$ shows why the two axes are not
interchangeable: at DRRA's settings the asymptote splits evenly between retained
KV columns ($12.5$ points) and index scoring ($12.5$ points), so a further step
on \emph{either} axis ($c_{KV}\,8{\to}16$ or $c_R\,4{\to}8$) buys the same $6.2$
points. That is the work-saving form of the decoupling result: the two axes are
equally powerful levers on cost, but only coarsening KV is (mildly) free in
accuracy on this task, so the decoupled corner takes the free lever and keeps the
costly one fine. The projection is deliberately coefficient-free---it is a ratio of
arithmetic counts---and it is a forecast, not a measurement: realized
energy savings will be smaller wherever memory traffic or fixed costs
dominate, as the batch-1 measurements already show.

The immediate
roadmap is temporal-dependence-aware statistics for the cross-dataset
suite, efficiency-oriented peer baselines, and hardware latency
benchmarking; each is itemized in
the appendix future-work list (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-roadmap}).
