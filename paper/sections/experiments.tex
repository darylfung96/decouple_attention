\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup and data integrity}
The factorial comprises $4$ compressed cells $\times$ $25$ paired seeds plus
the full-attention baseline at the same $25$ seeds $= 125$ runs under the
frozen protocol of Section~\ref{sec:task}. The five pre-registered seeds
$\{42, 123, 2024, 7, 99\}$ were run first (audited manifest with
parameter-equalization and training-match checks); a 20-seed power extension
(seeds 1--21 excluding the pre-registered set) was run later under the
byte-identical protocol and merged post hoc (seed list fixed in the extension design before any extension run; the merge was planned at that time). Three cells ($(4,4)$, $(4,8)$, $(8,8)$)
were subsequently re-run under a corrected index-to-block mapping (the original applied
one mapping rule to all geometries; Section~\ref{sec:family-mech}) and merged by seed,
replacing their rows; the $(8,4)$ and Normal rows are untouched originals. Data-integrity checks on the
merged manifest confirm: (i) parameter equalization within tolerance
(max attention delta $0.268\%$, max full-model delta $0.074\%$);
(ii) matched training across all runs (final training MSE span
$0.2756$--$0.3029$); and (iii) all 125 runs present, 25 seeds in every
cell, no duplicates. Reported validation and test metrics come from the
epoch-10 weights; no best-validation checkpoint selection is used. The 25 seeds capture training randomness on the fixed dataset and protocol, not future-period or dataset uncertainty.

\subsection{RQ1: the $2\times2$ factorial}
\label{sec:rq1}
Table~\ref{tab:factorial} and Figure~\ref{fig:factorial} summarize
validation MSE by cell over 25 paired seeds. No single cell dominates: the best
means are $(8,8)$ ($0.6128 \pm 0.0660$), CSA $(4,4)$ ($0.6148 \pm 0.0704$), and
DRRA $(8,4)$ ($0.6190 \pm 0.0827$)---all within seed noise of each other---while
$(4,8)$ is the worst compressed cell ($0.6810 \pm 0.0570$). Every compressed cell
beats the full-attention baseline on mean validation and mean test MSE; per-seed,
22--25 of 25 paired comparisons favor each compressed cell (95/100 in total). Head-to-head on validation against CSA, DRRA wins 16/25 and (8,8) wins 12/25 while (4,8) wins 6/25; best-of-four counts are 8 CSA, 8 (8,8), 7 DRRA and 2 (4,8), so there is no best cell. On test the same DRRA-vs-CSA contrast favors DRRA by -0.0331 (95% CI [-0.070, +0.004], exact p = 0.032, 17/25; best-of-four 12/25 for DRRA), reported as a secondary unadjusted contrast alongside the pre-registered validation analysis.

\textbf{Main effects.} Averaging over the opposite axis (Figure
\ref{fig:main-effects}): coarsening KV compression from $4{:}1$ to $8{:}1$
\emph{lowers} validation MSE by $-0.0320$ (95\% CI $[-0.062, -0.002]$; mildly beneficial),
while
coarsening routing resolution from $4{:}1$ to $8{:}1$ \emph{raises} it by
$+0.0300$ (95\% CI $[+0.001, +0.059]$; deterioration; Wilcoxon $p = 0.059$,
paired $t$ $p = 0.043$). Signs are consistent in 20 of 25 (KV) and 16 of 25
(routing) seed-column comparisons. The interaction is significant:
$-0.0724$ (95\% CI $[-0.129, -0.016]$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.014$), and reading it as
conditional effects localizes it. Per seed, coarsening KV compression is nil at
fine routing ($+0.0042$, 95\% CI $[-0.044, +0.052]$, exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.62$,
9/25) and beneficial at coarse routing ($-0.0682$, $[-0.101, -0.036]$,
$p = 0.0001$, 21/25), while coarse routing costs accuracy at fine KV content
($+0.0662$, $[+0.027, +0.106]$, $p = 0.002$, 19/25) and is nil at coarse content
($-0.0062$, $[-0.048, +0.035]$, $p = 1.0$, 13/25). The interaction is therefore carried by the
$(4,8)$ cell: at $c_{KV}{=}8$ the finer router does not improve the
mean at all, so the two axes are \emph{not} additive and the main effects are not
available as general mechanism statements---they must be read conditionally, and
the routing main effect in particular is a statement about the fine-content
configuration (paired per-seed intervals for all four conditional effects, and
for the two main effects and the interaction, are in the released analysis log).

\textbf{Pre-registered hypothesis H1} asked whether coarsening the router
deteriorates performance \emph{more} than coarsening KV compression. At
$n{=}25$ paired seeds the strict form is \emph{supported}: on per-seed
main-effect differences the routing effect exceeds the KV effect by $+0.0620$ on
average (exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.0074$; paired $t$-test $p = 0.0107$;
full recomputation from unrounded seeds in the released analysis log). This
per-seed contrast is one of four reported estimates (the two main effects, the
interaction, and the direct DRRA-vs.-equal-rate difference below)---it pairs all
four $(c_{KV}, c_R)$ observations within a seed, and it is algebraically identical
to the off-diagonal difference $y_{4,8} - y_{8,4}$, so the off-diagonal alone carries
no independent information. Both individual effects remain modest relative to seed noise (${\sim}0.03$ vs.\ mean
within-cell std $0.070$, per-cell range $0.057$--$0.083$; sign consistency
20/25 (KV) and 16/25 (routing)), and --- with a significant interaction and no single
best cell --- we report the decoupling as a \emph{statistically supported sign structure
with non-additive axes} whose mechanism-level reading is still
interpretation (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}; the mixed-effects model
$\mathrm{MSE} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \log_2 c_{KV} + \beta_2 \log_2 c_R +
\beta_3 \log_2 c_{KV}\log_2 c_R + \text{random effects}$ is deferred as a robustness re-expression (the $2\times2$ with seed blocking already
identifies all three fixed effects; more rate levels would add shape, not
identification)). Per-seed trajectories show the
ordering across cells is not unanimous for every seed---with the top three cells tied
within noise, there is no ``best'' cell to claim at all.

\textbf{What the $c_{KV}$ contrast changes.} The factorial compares complete
configurations at a fixed selection budget $k{=}16$: coarsening KV compression also
coarsens the span each selection covers, so realized coverage is not held fixed
(the measured unique selections are about 14 of 64 blocks for equal-rate cells
and about 10 of 32 for the dual-rate cell; Section~\ref{sec:method}). The KV main
effect is therefore a statement about the configuration---content density at a
fixed selection count---and not about content density at matched realized
coverage. Budget-matched controls that fix the number of unique valid blocks
after mapping, and coverage-matched sensitivity analyses across $c_{KV}$, are the
natural next comparisons and are deferred (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-roadmap}).

\textbf{Cross-dataset replication of the factorial.} The full four-cell factorial was
repeated on three contrasting datasets (ETTh2, Electricity, Exchange; five seeds each,
same protocol and corrected mapping). Cell means (CSA/DRRA/C48/C88 validation MSE):
ETTh2 1.78/1.81/1.69/1.99; Electricity 0.378/0.381/0.387/0.380 (all within 0.009);
Exchange 3.14/3.24/3.09/3.09. The ETTh1 sign structure does not travel: on ETTh2
the H1 contrast is $-0.1217$ (opposite sign from ETTh1; exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.63$), on
Exchange it is $-0.1446$ ($p = 0.81$), and on Electricity all four cells sit within 0.009
of each other (H1 $+0.0058$, $p = 0.0625$ --- too small to call). DRRA-vs.-CSA is a tie on
all three (W/L 2/3, 2/3, 3/2; all $p \ge 0.6$). The decoupling is therefore an ETTh1
finding, not a general design law (no cell leads best-of-four on any of the three datasets) --- consistent with the task-dependent reading of
Section~\ref{sec:rq3}.


\textbf{Exploratory probe: pushing KV compression to 16:1.} Motivated by the flat
KV-rate profile above, one further cell, $(16,4)$ (16 KV blocks, 64 index tokens),
was run post hoc on ETTh1 (25 seeds, same protocol; ranks held fixed with a disclosed
$+0.5\%$ parameter delta). It does not join the confirmatory contrasts (shown starred for reference), but the outcome
bounds the claim from above: $(16,4)$ reaches $0.5810 \pm 0.0751$ validation ($0.8100$
test), the best mean of any cell and ahead of Normal in all 25 seeds. Coarser KV
content is not merely free here --- it is mildly beneficial, consistent with the
negative KV main effect; the knee, if any, lies beyond 16:1. A further exploratory
probe at $(32,4)$ (8 KV blocks) lands at $0.6122 \pm 0.0652$ validation ($0.8586$ test),
still inside the band (beating Normal 24/25): the profile stays flat out to 32:1, and 4 KV blocks is the
sensible floor where this study stops.

\subsection{RQ2: compressed vs.\ full attention}
\label{sec:rq2}
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} compares full causal attention (Normal, 256
columns, no compression) with the two cells that anchor the family's
design corners, CSA $(4,4)$ and DRRA $(8,4)$.
Every compressed cell beats Normal on mean validation
and mean test MSE, and \emph{95 of the 100 paired seed comparisons}
favor the compressed configuration (per-seed validation deltas span
$-48.3\%$ to $+9.9\%$ across cells; mean deltas $-18.8\%$ to $-27.0\%$).
DRRA $(8,4)$ beats CSA $(4,4)$ head-to-head in 16 of 25 seeds on validation (CSA ahead in 9),
and no cell is best of all four in more than 8 (DRRA 7, $(8,8)$ 8, CSA 8, $(4,8)$ 2;
merged corrected-mapping analysis in the released logs). The advantage does not
depend on the dense baseline's wider output projection: retrained with the
compressed cells' rank-64 output projection (25 paired seeds, same protocol),
the dense model improves to $0.7910$ validation MSE but every compressed cell
still beats it (paired validation deltas $-0.110$ to $-0.178$ MSE, all four
95\% intervals excluding zero, $21$--$25$ of $25$ seeds per cell;
Section~\ref{sec:stats}).

\textbf{Wiring verification.} Because the compressed variants beating an
unmodified baseline by ${\sim}18$--$26\%$ is a surprising direction, we
audited the Normal attention implementation before reporting it: a
from-scratch naive reference (true causal masking, $1/\sqrt{d}$ scaling,
independent RoPE reimplementation) matches the module output
bit-exactly at $L{=}32$ and $L{=}64$ ($0.0$ max absolute difference) and to
$1.19 \times 10^{-7}$ at $L{=}256$ (fp32 accumulation noise); future
positions receive exactly zero weight and future-token perturbation leaves
outputs unchanged. The baseline is correctly implemented, so the comparison
stands. We also note the direction is surprising in the other sense:
compression acting as a task-appropriate inductive bias (see
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}) is a plausible but \emph{unverified}
explanation \emph{that three mechanistic probes now support descriptively}
(models retrained with per-run checkpoints under the same frozen protocol;
seed 42; regenerated against the current implementation after the mapping
correction): (i) on the delayed-copy dataset
(five channels periodic with in-window lags 96--256, plus two noise
channels), \emph{all three} models learn the exact-offset copy ---
per-channel copy MSE $0.0007$ (Normal) and $0.0013$--$0.00318$ (compressed
cells) against a noise floor of ${\approx}1.05$ --- so exact-offset recall
is not the differentiator, though Normal is ${\sim}2\times$ more precise
on the copy channels, with a mild lag gradient for the compressed cells;
(ii) on the change-point dataset, CSA $(4,4)$ has the lowest per-window error
in \emph{every} distance-from-change bucket (the across-bucket gradient is
confounded by the generator's random-walk drift and should not be
over-read); and (iii) across the twelve datasets, the compression
advantage correlates with signal smoothness in the predicted direction
(Pearson $r = -0.29$ for DRRA $(8,4)$, $-0.35$ for CSA $(4,4)$ against lag-1
autocorrelation; $p \approx 0.3$) --- smoother series favor compression
more; and (iv) a route-stability probe (one $(8,4)$ run, seed 42, on a fixed
validation batch) shows what the gradient-free selector does over training:
the selected sets are \emph{not} fixed (mean Jaccard overlap between the
first and final epoch $0.83$, with $37\%$ of positions unchanged), while the
routed \emph{amount} is --- the selected-set size holds at about $10$ of $32$
blocks and the route-score entropy stays flat ($3.13$) across all ten epochs
--- so membership follows the drift of upstream features and the routing
amount does not. These probes are descriptive ($n{=}1$ seed for (i)--(ii)
and (iv), $n{=}12$ for (iii)) and do not establish the mechanism.

\subsection{RQ3: cross-dataset coverage}
\label{sec:rq3}
To test whether the ETTh1 result is a single-dataset artifact, we repeated
the three-way comparison (Normal vs.\ CSA $(4,4)$ vs.\ DRRA $(8,4)$) on eleven
further datasets---three more ETT members, three large multivariate sets,
two different-dynamics sets, and three mechanistic synthetic families---under
the identical frozen protocol with the same five paired seeds (180 runs in
total; per-seed tables in the released results manifests). Table~\ref{tab:coverage} and
Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} summarize validation MSE by dataset. The ETTh1
column reflects the suite's five-seed run; the factorial
(Table~\ref{tab:factorial}) reports the same configuration at 25 seeds, and
the five pre-registered ETTh1 seeds are shared bit-identically between the
two manifests.

\textbf{Reading the table.} Table~\ref{tab:coverage} is transposed relative
to the earlier ones: each \emph{row} is a configuration (Normal, CSA $(4,4)$,
DRRA $(8,4)$, plus a $\Delta$val row for DRRA $(8,4)$ vs.\ Normal) and each
\emph{column} is one of the twelve datasets; a cell holds the mean
validation MSE over the five paired seeds, with the best mean per dataset
column in bold. Reading down a column compares the three models on one
dataset---e.g., ETTm2 is the suite's largest win for DRRA $(8,4)$ ($-48.4\%$),
while in the syn-delayedcopy column all three models agree within $0.001$
and Normal's mean is best. Reading across a row compares one model across
datasets, but \emph{columns are not comparable to each other}: every
dataset is standardized with its own train-only statistics, so MSE values
only have meaning within a column. The final $\Delta$val row is
scale-free (a ratio per dataset) and is therefore the row to compare across
columns; it also carries the notable exceptions---positive entries on
Exchange ($+8.6\%$) and on all three synthetic families ($+0.2\%$ to
$+3.1\%$).

The compressed configurations beat full attention on mean validation MSE in
8/12 datasets each (DRRA $(8,4)$ and CSA $(4,4)$), but the advantage is
\emph{signal-dependent} rather than universal. Best-mean columns split evenly:
4 datasets each for CSA, DRRA, and full attention. The largest wins are on the
ETT family (up to $-48.4\%$ on ETTm2) and on solar-energy; both compressed
variants \emph{lose} on all three synthetic families ($+0.2\%$ to $+4.0\%$)
and on exchange-rate validation, and their test error is worse on weather
(CSA $+11.2\%$, DRRA $+14.2\%$). This pattern is consistent with the
compression-as-inductive-bias reading: pooling adjacent timesteps is nearly
lossless on smooth, temporally redundant real-world series, while tasks
requiring exact in-window content recall (the delayed-copy probe,
known-period sinusoids) leave full attention with a slight edge. These
observations remain descriptive: five paired seeds per dataset, no
significance test, and the three rows are not parameter-equalized
($\sim$2\% full-model spread; the same stance as
Section~\ref{sec:rq2}).

\subsection{External reference points}
\label{sec:refpoints}
The comparisons above are internal to the family: every row shares one
backbone and differs only in the attention module. To make the absolute
MSE levels interpretable, we additionally ran two widely used published
forecasting architectures under the \emph{identical frozen protocol} (task,
split, train-only normalization, optimizer, schedule, five paired seeds) as
\emph{reference points}, not as peer methods: \textbf{DLinear}
\citep{zeng2023dlinear} (channel-shared configuration) and \textbf{PatchTST}
\citep{nie2023patchtst} (official supervised ETTh1 configuration: patch 16,
stride 8, $d_{\mathrm{model}}=16$, 3 layers, dropout 0.3, RevIN). Their edge
over our minimal single-block backbone comes from their full architecture
(patching, channel independence, decomposition; in DLinear's case no
attention at all), not from their attention; the comparison is therefore
confounded by design, and \emph{no competitiveness claim is made or
implied}. The purpose is calibration: what an interested reader needs in
order to locate the family's absolute numbers. Because the protocol is held
fixed while the architectures are not peers, the anchors are
protocol-comparable but not method-comparable.

Table~\ref{tab:refpoints} reports the result. The anchors are decisively
ahead on the real-world sets---most starkly, DLinear (49K parameters, no
attention) outperforms every configuration of the family on ETTh1 validation
and test. This is the LTSF-linear result of \citet{zeng2023dlinear}
reproduced under our frozen protocol, and it re-anchors the paper's reading:
at this scale, the attention module is not the accuracy bottleneck, and the
design-space structure measured in RQ1--RQ3 is a property of the attention
family itself, orthogonal to backbone competitiveness. Consistent with this,
the internal ordering of the family (compressed $>$ full attention on most
datasets; decoupled-rate effects) is a statement within the shared backbone
and is unchanged by the anchors' presence. PatchTST is reported only where
its channel-independent training fits the single-machine CPU budget (the
ETT family, exchange, and the synthetic probes); on the wide datasets
(solar 137, electricity 321, traffic 862 channels) it is a \emph{declared
omission} (${\sim}3$--$13$\,h per seed on CPU), not a missing result (solar, electricity, traffic and weather omitted for the same budget reason).

The probes also bound the anchors' advantage from the other side: on two of the
three mechanistic synthetic families---delayed-copy and known-period sinusoids---the
ordering \emph{inverts}, and both
anchors fall behind even full attention (delayed-copy: Normal $0.289$ vs.\
DLinear $0.408$ and PatchTST $0.342$; known-period sinusoids: Normal
$0.123$ vs.\ DLinear $0.251$). The change-point family is excluded from this reading:
there the anchors are far better than every family member (Normal $5.9456$ vs.\
DLinear $0.0622$, PatchTST $0.0489$), so no inversion holds there. A PatchTST-backbone transfer with the same four cells grafted on finds all four tied within noise on ETTh1/ETTh2 (Appendix; forward latency DRRA 1.11x and (8,8) 1.27x vs CSA at tied accuracy); that transfer tokenizes the series into 31 patches, where the coarse-router cells can select every candidate index token, so the tie bounds the transfer claim from below rather than establishing that the rate effects vanish on stronger backbones (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-exploratory}). Tasks requiring exact in-window content
recall are exactly where attention helps relative to linear or patched
architectures---consistent with the compression-as-inductive-bias reading
of Section~\ref{sec:rq3}, and worth further study.

\subsection{Statistical treatment}
\label{sec:stats}
Statistical status is reported separately for the two levels of the study.

\textbf{Factorial (ETTh1, $n{=}25$ paired seeds; full analysis log released
with the source).} At 25 paired seeds, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests on validation MSE are powered: \emph{every} compressed cell beats
full attention, and the result survives Holm correction across the five
pre-registered cell-vs-Normal contrasts (Holm-adjusted $p \le 3.3\times10^{-6}$
for all four cells). The pre-registered H1 contrast (per-seed routing
effect minus KV effect) is likewise significant (exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.0074$;
paired $t$ $p = 0.0107$). The DRRA-vs.-equal-rate edge is \emph{not} significant
on validation (DRRA vs.\ CSA: exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.62$; 16/25 head-to-head; no cell
best of all four in more than 8 of 25), and ``best on mean'' is no longer claimed at all.
On \emph{test} MSE the same contrast favors DRRA (mean $-0.0331$,
95\% CI $[-0.070, +0.004]$, exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.032$; 17/25 head-to-head; best of
four in 12/25 for DRRA)---a secondary, unadjusted contrast reported alongside the
pre-registered validation analysis. The $n{=}25$ set is a post hoc merge of the five
pre-registered seeds with a 20-seed power extension run under the
byte-identical protocol (plus the corrected-mapping re-runs of three cells under the
same protocol); the seed list was fixed in the extension's design
before any extension run, and the merge was planned at that time.

\textbf{Cross-dataset suite ($n{=}5$ paired seeds per dataset; analysis log
released with the source).} Per-dataset, the three comparisons
(DRRA vs.\ Normal, CSA $(4,4)$ vs.\ Normal, DRRA vs.\ CSA) were tested with
exact paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests; \emph{nothing survives Holm
correction across the 36 tests}, and with $n{=}5$ the two-sided floor is
$p = 0.0625$, so no per-dataset effect can reach two-sided $p < 0.05$ (the closest
raw values are etth1 and solar at $0.0625$ in favor of DRRA $(8,4)$). The two exact-recall
probes reach significance against compression one-sided (exact $p = 0.03125$: all five
seeds favor full attention on syn-sinusoids and syn-delayedcopy); syn-changepoints is not
significant in either direction. Across
datasets, paired sign tests on the 12 dataset means are not significant:
8/12 ($p = 0.39$) for DRRA $(8,4)$ and 8/12 ($p = 0.39$) for CSA $(4,4)$.
The strongest scale-free aggregate statement is the bootstrap 95\% CI on
the mean of the per-dataset $\Delta$val vs.\ Normal, which excludes zero
for both variants (CSA $(4,4)$: $-7.5\%$ $[-15.8, -0.9]$; DRRA $(8,4)$:
$-8.3\%$ $[-17.5, -0.8]$). The cross-dataset pattern is therefore
directionally consistent but not statistically established per dataset:
the ETTh1 factorial, not the suite, is where significance is established (25 paired seeds versus 5 per dataset).
Deferred items, with written justification in the project plan: the
mixed-effects model (Section~\ref{sec:rq1}), HAC/block-bootstrap
corrections (the analyses are at the seed level, where the paired runs are
independent, so stride-1 window overlap does not invalidate the paired
tests), and TOST/ROPE (the paper makes no equivalence claims).

\textbf{Sequence-length scaling and complexity.}
Figure~\ref{fig:lscale} sweeps the lookback $L \in \{128, \dots, 4096\}$ at
batch 1 and 32. At batch 32 DRRA $(8,4)$'s advantage over Normal grows from
$-32\%$ at $L{=}512$ to $-56\%$ at $L{=}2048$ ($869$ vs.\ $1984$ ms/batch, a
$2.3\times$ speedup), and at batch 1/$L{=}4096$ it reaches $-63\%$ ($86$ vs.\
$235$ ms)---while at $L \le 256$ and batch 1 Normal is fastest (fixed overheads
again). The
empirical log-log slopes ($L \ge 512$) are $1.40$ (DRRA $(8,4)$), $1.42$
(CSA $(4,4)$), and $1.72$ (Normal) at batch 32: consistent with the complexity
analysis of Section~\ref{sec:factorial}---the routing term
$O(L^2/c_R)$ keeps every cell asymptotically quadratic, with a reduced
constant for the compressed cells, and Normal approaches the quadratic
slope fastest. We therefore do \emph{not} claim subquadratic scaling. At
the extreme, batch-32/$L{=}4096$ full attention requires a ${\sim}8$ GB
score tensor and was skipped on the 16 GB machine. That ${\sim}8$ GB wall is a
property of our materializing implementation, not of dense attention as such:
fused kernels (e.g.\ FlashAttention \citep{dao2022flashattention}) avoid
materializing the score tensor and move the wall elsewhere---so the memory
advantage reported here is implementation-relative, and GPU transfer may erase it. All models
process the frozen task in under $2$ ms per sequence at batch 32 (mean
$1.6$--$1.8$ ms/seq at $L{=}256$; the $L{\ge}512$ region of the scaling
panel is where attention, not fixed overhead, dominates per-batch time).

\textbf{Compute and memory accounting.} Analytic forward MACs per sequence
(L=256, dominant terms; closed-form accounting in the released benchmark
scripts):
Normal $76.9$M, CSA $(4,4)$ $63.3$M, DRRA $(8,4)$ $61.2$M --- DRRA $(8,4)$ needs
$20\%$ fewer MACs than Normal. Three caveats bound that number. First, the
as-run rows are
\emph{not} projection-matched: the dense baseline carries a rank-128 output
projection ($21.0$M) where the compressed cells use the equalized rank-64
bottleneck ($10.5$M), and that one asymmetry is $10.5$M of the $15.7$M
whole-model gap. Matching the projection ranks leaves a $7.8\%$ whole-model
difference, while the attention path itself falls $30.9\%$ and the attention core
falls $80.9\%$ (Section~\ref{sec:forecast}). We therefore retrained the dense
baseline with the matched projection (25 paired seeds, same protocol): matching
the rank makes the dense model \emph{better}, not worse ($0.7910$ vs $0.8389$
validation MSE; paired mean $-0.048$, 95\% interval $[-0.088,-0.008]$), so the
as-run Normal was not a conservative reference. Every compressed cell still
beats the matched baseline, with paired validation deltas $-0.110$ to $-0.178$
MSE whose 95\% intervals all exclude zero ($21$--$25$ of $25$ seeds per cell),
and the ordering is the same on test MSE. The accuracy advantage is therefore
not a projection artifact: matching the projection removes the MAC wedge and
leaves the accuracy gap.
Second, the wrapper MLP
($33.9$M) is the largest single block for \emph{every} model, so attention
is not the dominant compute at this scale. Third, measured latency differences
($-6\%$) are smaller than the MAC difference because dense-masked
CPU GEMMs are efficient relative to the extra compressor/indexer work.
And MACs are not energy: arithmetic counts omit memory traffic and
hardware-specific efficiency, so any of these MAC gaps is a work statement, not an
energy claim (the deployment reading in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} is explicitly
conditional on hardware where attention dominates).
Peak resident memory during a batch-32 forward burst grows by
${\sim}0.3$ GB (process watermark; parameter memory is only ${\sim}1.4$ MB
per model), and at batch-32/$L{=}4096$ the full-attention score tensor
(${\sim}8$ GB) exceeds the 16 GB machine. Training time over the fixed
10-epoch ETTh1 budget (parsed from the sweep log; medians of 5 runs):
Normal $474$ s, CSA $(4,4)$ $511$ s, DRRA $(8,4)$ $459$ s --- one DRRA run
($1417$ s) overlapped a machine-sleep window and is excluded from the
median. All latency/compute measurements are single-machine (Apple M2,
fp32, 8 threads); cross-hardware transfer (Linux x86, GPU) and model
depth/width sweeps remain future work (Appendix future-work item~4).

\subsection{Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}
Table~\ref{tab:configs} gives the structural efficiency story: DRRA $(8,4)$
attends to 48 columns per query (5.22$\times$ fewer complete columns than Normal)
at 95{,}652 attention parameters---\emph{fewer} than Normal's 102{,}496---and
a full-model count 2.0\% below Normal's. Routing cost is identical for the
two $c_R{=}4$ cells regardless of $c_{KV}$, which is the deliberate
trade-off: pay the full fine-routing cost, halve the attention cost.

Table~\ref{tab:speed} reports wall-clock forward-pass latency measured
\emph{now} under the frozen protocol on the development machine, using the
alternating head-to-head protocol (all three models timed in rotation within
each round, so each round shares the same thermal/contention window; 25
rounds of 30 passes after warm-up; mean over per-round medians with 95\%
percentile bootstrap CIs, plus p90/p99 tails). DRRA $(8,4)$ has the lowest
mean latency ($49.3$ ms vs.\ $52.5$ ms for Normal, $-6.1\%$; CSA $(4,4)$
$-0.7\%$), and the ordering $(8,4) < (4,4) <$ Normal replicated across a
second run, but the 95\% CIs overlap and the level shifts by
${\sim}6$--$9\%$ between runs (thermal and contention effects), so we report
the latency comparison as \emph{directional} only. Paired within-round analysis tightens
this without changing the stance: in a 25-round round-robin rerun under the frozen protocol
(released audit log), DRRA $(8,4)$ beats Normal by $-9.0 \pm 1.7$ ms per paired round
and CSA $(4,4)$ by $-5.2 \pm 0.9$ ms (95\% CIs exclude zero \emph{in that run}); absolute
levels still drift across runs, so directional-only stands. A last-query audit reframes
the comparison further: the forecast uses only the last position (Section~\ref{sec:task}),
and recomputing latency for that forecast-required path (bit-exact to ${\sim}5\times10^{-7}$)
gives Normal $2.6$--$3.9$ ms, CSA $(4,4)$ $6.2$--$9.5$ ms, DRRA $(8,4)$ $5.8$--$8.6$ ms per
batch-32 pass---ranges span two runs showing the same level drift. Under last-query accounting
the dense baseline's last-query path is the fastest, because it pays no
compressor/indexer work: Table~\ref{tab:speed} compares full-layer costs, both costs are
reported, and efficiency claims are stated against the last-query baseline. The exploratory
cells were timed the same way: full-layer $(16,4)$ and $(32,4)$ run at $1.22\times$ Normal
(37.3--37.4 ms vs.\ 45.6 ms), barely ahead of $(8,4)$ at $1.18\times$ --- wall-clock plateaus
past $(16,4)$ because compressor work and dense masking dominate the shrinking column
count, so further compression buys accuracy-neutrality, not speed. Notably, the structural
5.22$\times$ column reduction does not translate into proportional wall-clock
gains at this scale: dense-masked CPU GEMMs are efficient and the
compressor/indexer add overhead. These are single-machine CPU measurements
(fp32, 8 threads); cross-hardware transfer (Linux x86, GPU) remains
follow-up work (Appendix future-work item~4).
The pilot-era speed figures from earlier stages of this project remain
superseded and are not used here.

\textbf{Batch-size scaling.} Figure~\ref{fig:latency-scale} shows how the
comparison changes with the number of input windows processed per forward
pass (mean ms per sequence). At batch size 1 the fixed per-model overheads
(compressor, indexer) dominate and Normal is fastest ($2.5$ vs.\ $3.3$
ms/seq for DRRA $(8,4)$, $+30.8\%$). From batch 8 onward the compressed cells run
at or below Normal, and at large batches the attention-column reduction
takes over: the DRRA $(8,4)$ mean advantage over Normal is $-6.1\%$ at batch 64,
$-5.1\%$ at 100, $-10.5\%$ at 128, and $-21.1\%$ at 256 ($3.49$ vs.\ $4.42$
ms/seq). At the mid-range batches 16--32 the two are within run-to-run
noise (the scaling run shows $-0.1$ to $+0.2\%$ there while the canonical
Table~\ref{tab:speed} run shows $-6.1\%$ at batch 32). The overall pattern
is the expected signature of amortized overheads: the compression machinery
costs fixed work per pass, while the saved attention columns scale with the
batch.

\textbf{Synthesis: is the dual-rate design better?} Each table speaks to one
axis of ``better.'' Table~\ref{tab:configs} establishes the \emph{structural}
side: DRRA $(8,4)$ needs 48 attention columns (5.22$\times$ fewer complete columns than Normal),
slightly fewer parameters than Normal, and the same fine-routing candidate
count as the equal-rate cells. Tables~\ref{tab:factorial}--\ref{tab:coverage}
establish the \emph{accuracy} side: DRRA $(8,4)$ beats Normal on mean
validation MSE in 8/12 datasets, and the top three factorial cells on ETTh1 sit
within seed noise of each other (no single best cell), with effects that are directional and, on the synthetic probes and some
datasets, reversed. Table~\ref{tab:speed} and Figure~\ref{fig:latency-scale}
establish the \emph{measured-latency} side: DRRA $(8,4)$ has the lowest mean
latency at batch sizes $\ge 64$ (to $-21\%$ at 256) but is not statistically
separated from Normal at batch 32, and is slower at batch 1. Read together,
the evidence favors DRRA $(8,4)$ on every axis on both backbones (Appendix transfer-speed figure shows the same pattern on PatchTST)---geometry, parameters, accuracy
on most datasets, large-batch latency---but none of the advantages clears a
significance or noise threshold on its own, and the accuracy advantage is
dataset-dependent. We therefore state the conclusion as \emph{directional
superiority under this protocol on most datasets}, not a universal or
statistically established claim.

\textbf{Combined performance--speed view.}
Figure~\ref{fig:perf-speed} shows performance and speed \emph{together},
without collapsing them into a single score: the y-axis is the mean test

\subsection{Scaling forecast and energy projection}
\label{sec:forecast}

The frozen task fixes the context at $L{=}256$, but deployed attention
systems increasingly run at context lengths of thousands to hundreds of
thousands of tokens. Re-measuring the protocol at every length of interest
would be costly and, more importantly, unnecessary: the cost of this
family is governed by a column geometry that is known in closed form and
has already been audited at $L{=}256$ (Section~\ref{sec:family-mech}).
We therefore use the audited component accounting to \emph{forecast}
attention work across the context range $L \in [256, 2^{17}]$ and to
translate that forecast into an explicit energy projection
(Figure~\ref{fig:scaling-forecast}). No model is retrained and no new run
is performed; the forecast is a statement about the geometry this paper
has already measured, and Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-energy} gives its
full derivation, sensitivity analysis, and audit.

\textbf{Three savings measures.} We keep three quantities separate,
because they answer different questions and differ by more than an order
of magnitude at some scales. The \emph{attention matrix} is the number of
score columns each query reads: $L$ for dense attention, and
$L/c_{KV} + W + 1$ for a compressed cell. The \emph{attention path} adds
the two costs the family introduces---the router, which compares each
query against $L/c_R$ index tokens, and the two-series compressors---so
that a compressed cell is charged for everything it executes. The
\emph{whole layer} adds the non-attention block (projections and MLP),
which the cell definition leaves unchanged. One column read costs
$2 d_h$ multiply--accumulate operations (MACs), one score and one value
read, and every count is fixed by the design and evaluated analytically;
Table~\ref{tab:notation} collects the notation with the frozen values.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\caption{Notation for the projection, with values for the frozen
configuration. The rates $c_{KV}$ and $c_R$ take the values listed per
cell in Table~\ref{tab:configs}.}
\label{tab:notation}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & Value \\
\midrule
$L$ & context length: past positions each query may read & $256$ \\
$c_{KV}$ & positions per compressed key--value block & $4$ or $8$ \\
$c_R$ & positions per routing index token & $4$ or $8$ \\
$W$ & uncompressed sliding window & $16$ \\
$+1$ & sink column & $1$ \\
$h$ & attention heads & $4$ \\
$d_h$ & head dimension & $32$ \\

\bottomrule

For a sequence of length $L$
the attention core of the dense baseline performs $W_N(L) = 2 h d_h L^2$
multiply--accumulate operations (scores and values; the study's frozen
geometry is $h{=}4$, $d_h{=}32$, $d{=}128$, $W{=}16$, with $d = h d_h$),
while a compressed cell with rates $(c_{KV}, c_R)$ performs
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cf-work}
W_C(L; c_{KV}, c_R)
= 2 h d_h L\!\left(\frac{L}{c_{KV}} + W + 1\right)
+ d \frac{L^{2}}{c_R}
+ 2 L d\,(64 + d_h),
\end{equation}
where the three terms price, in order, the score--value computation over
the fixed column set, the router, and the two-series compressors. The
first covers the $L/c_{KV}$ compressed blocks, the $W$ uncompressed
window positions, and the sink column per query, at $2 d_h$ MACs per
column per head, where the factor $L$ counts queries, each paying for the
same menu; the second scores all $L/c_R$ index tokens per query with every
head of the shared routing query, at $h d_h = d$ MACs per comparison
(the routing query is the main multi-head query, so one head's dot product
per token pair would under-count by $h$; the executed tensor shapes are
recorded in the released audit log), leaving the routing cost quadratic in $L$
with the reduced constant $1/c_R$; the third is the per-token cost of
building both compressed series, $2d(64 + d_h)$ MACs, the only term
linear in $L$. That linear term dominates at short context---6.29M of
the 11.60M attention-path MACs of DRRA $(8,4)$ at $L{=}256$ (the router
contributes 2.10M), hence its
30.9\% saving there---and is asymptotically negligible.
The corresponding attention-path saving,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cf-savings}
S(L) = 1 - \frac{W_C(L)}{W_N(L)},
\qquad
S_\infty = \lim_{L \to \infty} S(L)
= 1 - \left(\frac{1}{c_{KV}} + \frac{1}{2 c_R}\right),
\end{equation}
is the fraction of attention-path work removed at length $L$. Here
$W_N(L) = 2 h d_h L^2$ is the dense attention work introduced above, and
$W_C(L)$ abbreviates the compressed attention-path work
$W_C(L; c_{KV}, c_R)$ of Equation~\ref{eq:cf-work}, so the ratio inside
$S(L)$ is the share of dense work the compressed path still spends. In
the limit only two terms survive---the retained columns perform
$1/c_{KV}$ of the dense attention work and the router contributes
$1/(2 c_R)$---because these are the coefficients of the two $L^2$ terms
of Equation~\ref{eq:cf-work}, while the window offset and the compressor
cost are
$O(1)$ and $O(L)$ against the $O(L^2)$ attention terms; for DRRA
$(8,4)$ this leaves $1/8 + 1/8 = 25.0\%$, or $75.0\%$ removed.
Dividing
Equation~\ref{eq:cf-work} by $W_N$ exposes the same decomposition at
finite context: with the frozen geometry ($d = h d_h$) the compressed
path pays four fixed shares of dense work,
$1/c_{KV} + (W+1)/L + 1/(2 c_R) + (64 + d_h)/L$, of which the two
$1/L$ shares shrink as context grows; in the evaluated form the
compressed path reads $W_C(L) = 256\,L\,(L/c_{KV} + 17) + 128\,L^2/c_R +
24{,}576\,L$, with $256 = 2 h d_h$, $17 = W + 1$, $128 = h d_h = d$, and
$24{,}576 = 2d(64 + d_h)$. At the frozen context the
savings are modest---18.4\% to 40.2\% across the four cells, because the
linear compressor term dominates at $L{=}256$---but they grow with
context and saturate at the limit: at $L{=}32{,}768$ the four cells
remove 62.2\%, 74.7\%, 80.9\%, and 84.0\% of attention-path work,
respectively, within a few tenths of a percentage point of $S_\infty$,
approaching it from below with the gap set by the compressor term
(Table~\ref{tab:forecast}). The map over the design plane
(Figure~\ref{fig:scaling-forecast}d) makes the asymmetry quantitative:
moving $c_{KV}$ from $4$ to $8$ at $c_R{=}4$ adds $12.5$ percentage
points of asymptotic saving, while moving $c_R$ from $4$ to $8$ at
$c_{KV}{=}8$ adds $6.2$---so the accuracy-favored axis is also the larger
saving lever, and keeping the router fine costs the smaller share
(Figure~\ref{fig:scaling-forecast}b, dashed lines).

\textbf{Whole-layer projection.} A saving on the attention path is not
the same as a saving on the layer, because the non-attention block is
unchanged by the cell definition. Holding that block fixed at the
standard per-token cost of $12 d^2$ multiply--accumulate operations
($4 d^2$ for the query, key, value, and output projections and $8 d^2$
for the MLP; at $d{=}128$ this reproduces the audited compressed-cell
non-attention accounting to within $1.5\%$, $49.60$M against $50.33$M
operations per sequence), the projected whole-layer saving is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cf-whole}
S_{\mathrm{layer}}(L; d) =
\frac{2dL - \left[\,2d\!\left(\frac{L}{c_{KV}} + W + 1\right)
+ d \frac{L}{c_R} + 2d(64 + d_h)\,\right]}{2dL + 12 d^{2}}.
\end{equation}
Both terms are per-token: the numerator is the attention-path work
removed, and the denominator the dense layer cost---attention $2dL$,
which is $2 h d_h L^2$ divided by $L$ since $d = h d_h$, plus the
unchanged $12 d^2$ block---so the ratio is the fraction of
whole-layer work removed. At a
$32$k-token context, DRRA $(8,4)$ removes $68.3\%$ of whole-layer
work at $d{=}512$, $62.9\%$ at $d{=}1024$, and $42.7\%$ at $d{=}4096$
(an 8B-class width), while the $(32,4)$ probe removes $76.8\%$,
$70.8\%$, and $48.0\%$; the fraction shrinks as $d$ grows because
attention is then a smaller share of layer work. For the study's own
backbone ($d{=}128$) the projected whole-layer saving is $50.5\%$ at
$L{=}2048$ and $60.8\%$ at $L{=}4096$.

The deeper KV cuts continue the trend with sharply diminishing returns at
the studied scale. The asymptotic attention-path saving rises from
$75.0\%$ for $(8,4)$ to $81.2\%$ and $84.4\%$ for $(16,4)$ and $(32,4)$;
at the frozen context the same cuts add $6.2$ and $9.3$ points
($37.1\%$ and $40.2\%$ against $30.9\%$), because the compressor cost is
invariant to $c_{KV}$, and the measured full-layer speedup plateaus at
$1.22\times$ for both probes against $1.18\times$ for $(8,4)$---a
difference within the run-to-run drift documented for these measurements.
Accuracy
gives no counterpart reason to promote the deeper rates: the probes are
exploratory (post hoc, outside the pre-registered grid) and their spread
is within seed noise; $(16,4)$ has the best mean of the three on both
endpoints ($0.5810$ validation and $0.8100$ test against $0.6190$/$0.8440$
for $(8,4)$ and $0.6122$/$0.8586$ for $(32,4)$, $25$ seeds each), yet
$(32,4)$---the deepest cut---improves on $(16,4)$ on neither, so coarser
KV compression is not accuracy-monotone and neither probe carries a
confirmatory claim (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-exploratory}). We therefore
keep $(8,4)$ as the named dual-rate corner and treat the deeper rates as
boundary probes. They remain relevant for extreme-context deployments,
where the column budget of Figure~\ref{fig:scaling-forecast}a dominates;
that evaluation is left to future work.

\textbf{From work to energy.} To connect these counts to energy without
claiming an energy measurement, we adopt the first-order model
$E(\theta) = \sum_k \varepsilon_k \, n_k(\theta)$, in which energy is
proportional to arithmetic work $n_k(\theta)$ with per-operation
coefficients $\varepsilon_k$. Here $\theta$ denotes the configuration
(cell and context length), $k$ indexes operation type, $n_k(\theta)$ is
the number of operations of type $k$ (the counts of
Equation~\ref{eq:cf-work}), and $\varepsilon_k$ is the energy per
operation of type $k$, a hardware rather than a model property. With a
single coefficient, $E \propto W$, so the savings in
Equations~\ref{eq:cf-savings} and~\ref{eq:cf-whole} \emph{are}
fractional energy savings: the
coefficient is common to the baseline and
to the compressed model and cancels in the ratio, leaving the headline
projections independent of processor and process node under the stated
model. Figure~\ref{fig:energy-composition} shows the composition this
leaves at the study operating point ($L{=}2048$): compression shrinks
the attention core from $72.7\%$ of dense per-token work to $18.8\%$
(CSA) and $9.7\%$ (DRRA), the router and compressor add $12.5\%$, and
the projections-and-MLP block is untouched at $27.3\%$, so the
whole-model saving is smaller than the attention-path
saving---$41.4\%$ versus $57.0\%$ for CSA and $50.5\%$ versus $69.5\%$
for DRRA. Two consistency checks on the
work-to-time linkage that any such model requires are available from the
systems measurements. First, the measured forward latencies rank with the
analytic attention-path work across the audited lookback sweep (Spearman
$\rho = 1.000$ at batch 32 and $0.992$ at batch 1). Second, at the
operating points where the two are commensurate, the whole-layer
projection tracks the measured end-to-end saving: at $L{=}2048$/batch 32
it predicts $50.5\%$ against $56.2\%$ measured, and at $L{=}4096$/batch 1
it predicts $60.8\%$ against $63.3\%$ measured. The measured saving now
exceeds the arithmetic projection by $2.5$--$5.7$ points, the opposite of
what a pure work model predicts; the likely source is that the
dense baseline is memory-bound at these contexts (the materialized score
tensor dominates its time), so its wall-clock cost is larger than its
MAC count---an effect the work model deliberately excludes, and the same
implementation-relative memory advantage that Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}
already reports. Two
sensitivities bound the model's assumptions
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-energy}): reweighting the router and
compressor terms by a factor of $0.5$ leaves the $32$k DRRA projection at
$81.1\%$ and by a factor of $2$ at $61.9\%$ (central case $74.7\%$), with the
direction unchanged; and the executed
router is the expensive arrangement---it scores every index token with
every head of the shared query, and a single-head indexer (as in a
Lightning-Indexer-style design, which this module does not use) would raise the
$32$k DRRA projection from $74.7\%$ to $84.0\%$ and the asymptote from
$75.0\%$ to $84.4\%$.

What is \emph{not} claimed is equally explicit. These are model-based
projections from arithmetic counts, not energy measurements
(Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}); they model arithmetic work only, so
memory stalls, frequency scaling, and leakage are outside the model; and
they inherit the single-machine, CPU-scale scope of the underlying audit.
The value of the projection is that its leading term is a
hardware-independent ratio---the work reduction---which any energy
evaluation of this family must account for, and which the measurements
above anchor in direction.
